Graphic Novel: Motherhood in Crisis

Graphic Novel: Motherhood in Crisis uses the format of a graphic novel to portray the emotional and social struggles mothers face under real-world pressures. Centered on the theme of "motherhood crisis," the work illustrates the tensions women experience between economic strain, family responsibilities, and societal expectations, highlighting the difficult balance between caring for their children and maintaining their own sense of self.While reading, my strongest impression was the work's authentic portrayal of emotions. Through close-up shots of facial expressions, oppressive compositions, and darker tones, the graphic novel intensifies the mother's exhaustion and sense of isolation. This visual expression is more impactful than words alone, making it easier to feel the character's psychological state. At the same time, the combination of images and text gives the story greater depth, making complex social issues feel more immediate and tangible. Furthermore, this work has also led me to reflect on the lack of social support systems. The problems many mothers face are not due to personal inadequacy, but rather a shortage of sufficient social resources and understanding. I also noticed the narrative structure of the work. It does not tell a single event in a linear fashion; instead, it presents the accumulating pressures of a mother's daily life through the juxtaposition of fragmented scenes. This structure makes pressure itself visible, allowing readers to experience the continuity and repetitiveness of the emotions, rather than just a single conflict.

Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It

This article highlights the reality of the renewed escalation of nuclear threats in the contemporary world through depictions of the risks of nuclear war and analysis of real-world cases, warning people not to continue treating nuclear war as an 'unthinkable' historical issue. Set against the backdrop of the Russia-Ukraine war, and drawing on emergency preparedness in the United States and Ukraine, intelligence assessments, and discussions on the potential use of 'tactical nuclear weapons, the article illustrates how nuclear conflict has shifted from a theoretical threat to a matter of real-world contingency planning.In the past, nuclear war was often viewed as a relic of the Cold War era, but through specific scenario simulations and policy preparations, this article makes readers realize that it remains a present reality in contemporary geopolitics—and is even being seriously discussed and simulated in certain conflicts. This shift from an abstract threat to concrete scenarios makes nuclear war no longer seem distant, but rather a “potential reality.”The article’s detailed description of the nuclear explosion process is particularly striking. It not only depicts the intense heat, shock waves, and fireball at the moment of the explosion, but also delves into the long-term consequences, such as radiation, disease, ecological destruction, and global food crises. This multi-layered narrative allows readers to see not only “instantaneous destruction” but also “decades-long disaster.” Compared to traditional news reporting, this approach is more cautionary and visually impactful.Although nuclear weapons were designed as a deterrent to prevent war, the article points out that in the event of a miscalculation or escalation, the so-called “balance of terror” could quickly break down, triggering an uncontrollable chain reaction. In the absence of effective international communication and arms control agreements, the risks are clearly mounting. Overall, this article not only describes the dangers of nuclear war but also serves as a reminder to readers that the nuclear threat has not disappeared; rather, it persists in today’s world in more complex and covert forms. It prompts people to reconsider the importance of international cooperation, arms control, and mechanisms for peace.
